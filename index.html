fast_forward 1. Why Limiting WIP Slashes Cycle Time

When a developer finishes a task and cannot pull a new one because the WIP limit is reached, they are forced to swarm on active blockers, conduct code reviews, or pair with QA. This collapses idle queue time—the dominant cause of late deliveries.

Rule: Stop Starting, Start Finishing. Never leave a blocked pull request to begin a new card.
tune 2. How to Enforce Column Limits in Jira

Set explicit column caps on your Kanban board: In Development (Max 5) and In Review/QA (Max 3). If a column turns red from a WIP breach, standup immediately pivots to swarming until the buffer is cleared.

Jira Action: Board Settings ➔ Columns ➔ Set Min/Max constraints to highlight bottleneck starvation.
rule 3. Little's Law Assumptions Check

Little's Law holds true under 3 critical preconditions: 1) Consistent arrival and departure rates, 2) Roughly uniform story sizing (INVEST compliant), and 3) Work items do not remain stalled forever in a 'parking lot' state.

Audit: Discard stories stalled > 30 days back to the backlog to keep lead-time distribution reliable.

Least Invasive ➔ Highest Intervention

Allow healthy struggle for 2–3 minutes during refinement or standup. Gives developers the psychological space to self-organize without immediate coach intervention.

2

State purely observed facts without judgement: “I notice we have 8 tickets in code review and only 1 in development. What does that mean for our sprint goal?”

3

Inquire into systemic trade-offs: “If we commit to this spike today, what existing commitment are we comfortable letting slip to protect quality?”

4

Offer a low-stakes timeboxed trial: “Can we run an experiment for just this sprint where we cap PR reviews at 24h and review the results in retro?”

5

Step in firmly only when psychological safety, Definition of Done, or ethical delivery guardrails are breached: “We cannot deploy this code with failing automated tests; let's swarm on the rollback.”
