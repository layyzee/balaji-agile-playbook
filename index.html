Making Work Visible: The 5 Thieves of Time

Dominica DeGrandis • Queuing theory & invisible context-switching hazards.

Flow Diagnostics

Unseen work is unmanageable. In high-performing engineering squads, delivery delays are rarely caused by slow typing; they are caused by the 5 Thieves of Time stealing flow in the background:

Thief 1 Too Much WIP

Too many items in progress; leads to cognitive thrashing and delayed lead times.

Thief 2 Unknown Dependencies

Waiting on architecture, external vendor stubs, or security audit sign-offs.

Thief 3 Unplanned Work

Emergency production defects, side requests, or mid-sprint scope injections.

Thief 4 Conflicting Priorities

Competing goals between Product, Engineering Debt, and Customer Support.

Thief 5 Neglected Work

Postponed refactorings, skipped automated tests, and stale documentation.

Scrum and XP from the Trenches

Henrik Kniberg's Pragmatic Delivery Principles

Pure theoretical Scrum fails without Extreme Programming (XP) technical disciplines. Kniberg established that autonomous squads thrive when sprint backlogs are treated as locked commitments, combined with pair programming, test-driven spikes, and definition of done enforced at every pull request boundary.

Project to Product

Dr. Mik Kersten's Flow Framework

Software delivery must be measured by business value flow rather than proxy activities. Track the 4 Flow Metrics across all value streams: Flow Velocity (features shipped), Flow Time (ideation to production), Flow Efficiency (active work vs wait states), and Flow Load (total concurrent WIP).

Diagnosing Visual Delivery Charts

Spot team dysfunction in Sprint Burndowns days before sprint review.

Anti-Pattern Diagnostics
1. The Cliffhanger High Risk

Zero points burned down for 9 days, then a vertical drop on Day 10.

Root Cause: Giant stories; batch testing.
Coach Cure: Slice stories ≤ 3 days; test as you go.
2. The Flatliner Blocked

Remaining points stay completely horizontal throughout the entire sprint.

Root Cause: Hidden external dependency deadlock.
Coach Cure: Trigger decoupling spike; stub APIs.
3. The Staircase of Doom Scope Creep

Line steps UP repeatedly mid-sprint as scope is silently injected.

Root Cause: PO adds unrefined tickets mid-sprint.
Coach Cure: Enforce 1-in-1-out trade-off rule.
4. The Late Surge QA Bottleneck

Dev finishes early, but QA column overflows and burns down late.

Root Cause: Mini-waterfall inside the sprint.
Coach Cure: Devs write automated tests; swarm QA.

6 Power Jira Automations

Enforce Little's Law and eliminate status friction automatically in Jira.

Jira Automation Rules
1. Blocker SLA Alert (>24h)

When ticket marked "Flagged", trigger Slack alert if unassigned for 24h.

status = 'Blocked' AND updated <= -24h
2. Subtask Status Auto-Rollup

When all child subtasks move to 'Done', auto-transition parent story to 'In Review'.

issue.subtasks.every(s => s.status == 'Done')
3. WIP Limit Cap Trigger

Alert Scrum Master if any single developer has > 2 stories in active 'In Progress'.

assignee = currentUser() AND status = 'In Dev'
4. PR Review SLA Warning

Ping review channel if ticket remains in 'Peer Review' for more than 4 business hours.

status = 'Review' AND status changed to 'Review' before -4h
5. Mid-Sprint Scope Creep Tag

Auto-tag stories added after Sprint Kickoff Day 1 as 'scope-injection' for retro analysis.

labels += 'scope-creep'
6. Zero-Spillover Audit Flag

On Sprint Close, auto-extract uncompleted stories into a retrospective spillover report.

Sprint in closedSprints() AND status != Done

Phase 1: Inception

PO refuses to slice a 21-point monolithic epic before sprint planning.

Interview Question: "How do you handle a Product Owner who insists an architectural story cannot be broken down and must enter the sprint as-is?"

⭐ Staff Scrum Master Model Answer Quantified ROI Framework

Situation: During Sprint 4 planning, our PO brought a 21-point monolithic 'Billing Gateway Migration' epic with no split criteria, risking a complete sprint spillover.

Task: As Scrum Master, my responsibility was to safeguard the team's WIP boundaries and sprint goal commitment without blocking critical product delivery.

Action: I convened an immediate 30-minute slicing spike using the Hamburger Method and Workflow Step pattern. We decoupled the read-only reporting path from the transactional execution pipeline, splitting the 21-point epic into four 5-point independently releasable vertical slices with automated contract mocks.

Result: The team committed to Slices 1 and 2, delivering both by Day 8 with zero production defects. Cycle time dropped from an estimated 14 days to 4.2 days, and stakeholders received working software on schedule.
